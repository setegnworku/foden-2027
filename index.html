The gap this visit fills

Africa's dairy cows are crossbred

Most recorded cows are taurine × indicine crosses: two subspecies in one genome.

Evaluation ignores this

Genomic evaluation still treats all cows as one population.

The right models are untested here

Ancestry-aware models were built for Europe, where gains are modest. They should matter most in Africa, yet have hardly been tested there.

This visit joins ILRI's simulator and data with Roslin's methods and national-scale tools to close that gap, and ends with a completed manuscript and a workflow ILRI's breeding programmes can use.

1 · The problem

Two subspecies in one cow

Crossbred cows

Most recorded dairy cows in East Africa are crosses of exotic Bos taurus and indigenous, largely Bos indicus cattle, such as Holstein × Boran.

Two subspecies

This is a cross between two subspecies, not two breeds as in Europe.

Untested where it matters

In Europe, ancestry-aware models gave modest gains because the breeds are close. In Africa they should matter most, yet they have hardly been tested there.

2 · The gap

Why one relationship matrix is not enough

Genomic evaluation still treats all cows as one population. Three things can go wrong:

Indigenous effects are poorly estimated

They are learned mostly from exotic-rich cows.

Indigenous-rich cows are misjudged

Their breeding values are over-dispersed when allele effects differ between origins.

The adaptive indigenous contribution is invisible

So it is not managed.

3 · Preliminary work

The groundwork is already done

With the guidance of Gregor Gorjanc, I built a simulator that mimics African production systems: admixed taurine × indicine cows with the origin of every allele known, and a spatial environment. The population structure is taken from Houaga et al. (2026): the real herd structure of Tanzanian smallholder data, where most herds keep one cow.

+0.08 to +0.10accuracy (simulated, interim)

Preliminary analysis: the idea works

The ancestry-aware model and the spatial field together raised the accuracy of breeding values. This result comes from one specific simulated population. The core of the visit is to test it across a broad range of parameters.

A lesson from real data

Indigenous ancestry can be lost between genotyping and the relationship matrix. Ancestry-aware evaluation needs a genotype pipeline that preserves it.

5 · What the visit delivers

Outcomes

What is new

  • New evidence: ancestry-aware evaluation tested across a broad range of African production systems, where it has hardly been tested.
  • A new approach: ancestry-aware evaluation combined with a spatial field for smallholder herds.
  • New tools: a national-scale workflow and a genotype protocol that preserves indigenous ancestry.

For ILRI

  • Fairer breeding values for crossbred cows.
  • Ready to implement in genetic evaluations in the AADGG countries that hold these data: Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania and Nigeria.
  • Relevant to farmers: the bull chosen for a cow accounts for its ancestry.

For the Roslin Institute

  • A demanding new test case for its methods.
  • A basis for co-supervised students and exchange visits.
